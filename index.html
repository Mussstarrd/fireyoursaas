That's exactly what these engines compute. No more copying the guy down the road, and no more hoping the number in your head still covers this year's insurance.

What is "SaaS fatigue" — and is a spreadsheet really the fix?

SaaS fatigue is what small business owners call the pile-up of rising subscription costs, unused seats and features, and lock-in — stop paying and your own data disappears. For a one-to-three-person shop, pricing, quoting and job costing is math, and a purpose-built Excel file does that math without a monthly fee. These engines are that file: price book, quote builder, job log and profit dashboard, validated by 750+ automated formula checks.

Is this an alternative to field-service software like Jobber or Housecall Pro?

For the pricing, quoting and job-costing side at a shop of one or two — yes, that's exactly the job it does. Subscription field-service platforms run $588–$1,788 a year and often gate the flat-rate price book behind an add-on; they earn their keep on scheduling and dispatch for bigger crews. If what you actually use is the price book and the quote math, one Excel file you own covers it for $75, once.

What do the "750+ automated checks" actually check?

The hard part of pricing isn’t one formula — it’s the web of them. Your wage feeds a loaded rate, the loaded rate feeds every task, parts feed markups, surcharges stack on top — and one wrong link anywhere means every price downstream quietly leaks money. I untangled that web so you don’t have to, and the 750+ checks prove every connection holds: type a $28/hr wage and the engine must answer exactly $114.09/hr loaded. Flip after-hours and the premium must land on the whole job. The margin flag must trip at exactly 45%. The quick quote and the customer quote must agree to the penny. That’s what you’re buying — every number wired to every other number, and the wiring proven — so the price that comes out protects your margin instead of guessing at it. If one connection fails, the file doesn’t ship.

Do I need anything besides Excel? Any macros to enable?

No. Zero macros, no login, no internet connection required. The engines run in desktop Excel or Google Sheets and work offline in a driveway. That’s deliberate: a lot of spreadsheet tools this deep are riddled with macros — .xlsm files that trip Excel's security warnings and get blocked or stripped by email filters. These are plain .xlsx files, small enough to email to your phone, your accountant, or the shop laptop with zero warnings and nothing to enable.
